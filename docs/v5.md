# v5: one engine, two kinds of customer

v5 serves two buyers with the same check, live monitoring and fix loop:

- **A company that uses an AI agent with its own customers** (a head of support or CX). Grapim finds the rules
  that AI breaks, before customers do, and keeps watching after launch.
- **An AI vendor that deploys its agent to clients** (a head of forward deployed or solutions engineering).
  Each client gets its own workspace. Grapim does the slow part of every rollout, proving the agent keeps that
  client's policies, and gives the vendor one view of every client.

It also adds the pieces a legal, risk or compliance team asks for: an inline guard, an evidence pack and a
public page.

## Two kinds of organization

Sign-up asks "What do you do?":

| Choice | Org | Home |
| --- | --- | --- |
| We use an AI agent with our customers | customer org | AI agents, as before |
| We sell an AI agent to companies | partner org | Clients |

A vendor's clients are customer orgs managed by the partner org, through the existing `partner_org_id`. Owners
and admins add them on the Clients page. Each client's policies, checks, conversations and audit log stay in
that client's workspace.

**Billing:** a client workspace is billed to its vendor. It has the vendor's plan, and usage across the vendor
and all its clients counts toward the vendor's limits (`payerOf` in `src/v3/billing.mjs`). On the Vendor plan
that is $1,500 a month with 5 AI agents included and $200 for each extra one. Billing shows only on the
vendor's org.

## Agents that take actions

Most new agents do things, not only answer: refund, cancel, apply a code, hand off. v5 judges the actions.

- **What it did.** An HTTP agent can return its tool calls next to its reply; the agent form asks where (`actions`, a dot
  path). The check, the re-check and live monitoring give them to the judge: a forbidden action breaks the rule even
  when the words are right, and so does a reply claiming an action that was not taken. Results show "What it did".
- **Test accounts.** When the client's material describes a test account (orders, bookings, dates), the check writes
  each message about those exact records, choosing the one on the rule's boundary, instead of records the agent cannot
  find.
- **Action guard.** `POST .../guard` with `{ action, context, message }` checks one tool call before it runs and
  returns allow or block with what to tell the customer. See [v5-guard.md](v5-guard.md).
- **Sample agent.** An AI vendor's "Open a sample client" opens Linden & Co., a made-up store whose agent acts on a test
  account (`/demo/store-agent`), with its policy, tickets, order-system notes and test account loaded.
  `examples/linden-agent` is the same agent as a standalone service, with the guard wired in.
- **Sample clients by industry.** The vendor picks the sample client's industry: an online store (Linden & Co.:
  refunds, orders), a digital bank (Quillby Bank, `/demo/agent/bank`: identity before account details, fraud and scams,
  disputes, no financial advice, third parties) or telehealth (Velmora Health, `/demo/agent/health`: emergencies, no
  medical advice, privacy, controlled medicines). Each ships a policy, API notes, a test member and past tickets
  (`src/v3/sample-bank`, `src/v3/sample-health`), and its agent reports every tool call, reads included, so a check
  can see an account read before identity was verified.

## Connecting without the operator

- **Saved keys.** The agent form takes the key the customer's AI needs (HTTP, OpenAI-compatible, Intercom, Zendesk) in a
  password field. It is encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a key derived from the server's pepper, stored in the agent's
  folder, referred to as `${secret:NAME}`, filled in only at call time and never shown again. Settings can replace it.
- **Network guard.** An internet-facing server refuses tenant-chosen addresses that resolve to its own network
  (localhost, the cloud metadata address, private ranges) and refuses redirects; `DELOMU_ALLOW_PRIVATE_TARGETS`
  overrides it. See docs/v3.md.

## The public page

A signed-out visitor at `/` sees Grapim itself: a still of the check results and three short clips (the rollout,
the client's sign-off, the clients list), all recorded in Grapim on the sample store and served from `src/v3/media`
at `/media/:file` (with byte ranges, which Safari needs for video). Clips play only while on screen and not at all
under `prefers-reduced-motion`, where they keep their controls. Plans are on `/pricing`. It claims no customers,
logos or numbers.

## Links opened without an account

Sign-off and public pages use `share_links` (database migration 5). Only a SHA-256 hash of each token is
stored, so a link is shown once, when it is made. Sign-off links expire after 30 days, and public links last
until the page is turned off. Revoking a link takes effect on its next request. A sign-off link takes one
decision, and the form is rate limited per client address and protected by its own CSRF cookie.

## What v5 does not do yet

- Connections cover HTTP, OpenAI-compatible, Intercom, Zendesk and commands. Others (Freshdesk, Salesforce) wait
  for a customer who uses them. The Intercom and Zendesk connectors follow the vendors' docs but have not yet run
  against a real workspace.
- All of this has run only on the demo data (the fictional Acme bot). No real customer has used it yet.
- The playbook stays inside one vendor. A library shared across vendors would need a separate agreement with
  every contributing client, so it is not built.
